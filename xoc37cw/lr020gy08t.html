Let's get started

This calculator helps you rebuild your vehicle mileage from records you already have: service invoices, inspections, your calendar, invoices and receipts. It keeps what you can prove separate from what is estimated, and nothing is treated as final until your tax pro reviews it.

What the labels mean

Documented Backed by a record made at the time (a mileage log, receipt, invoice or calendar entry).

Reconstructed Rebuilt from records you can point to. Your tax pro reviews these.

Insufficient support Something is missing, like the business reason or a record. Not counted in deduction figures.

Approved for reporting Your tax pro reviewed it and it meets the record-keeping rules. Only your tax pro can set this.

Your vehicles

Add each vehicle used for business during the year. Each one is calculated on its own.

Odometer readings

Enter every odometer reading you can find, in any order: oil changes, tire service, inspections, registration renewals, purchase or sale paperwork, or a photo of the dashboard. The calculator sorts them, finds the miles between each one, and estimates January 1 and December 31 when you don't have a reading on those days.

Business trips

List business trips with where you went and why. Pull them from your calendar, client meetings, invoices, receipts, delivery records or an existing mileage log. Every business trip needs a business reason.

What counts as business driving?

Usually business: driving to meet clients, to job sites or temporary work locations, to the bank or supply store for the business, and between two work locations. Driving from your home office to other business stops can count when your home is your main place of business.

Usually not business: the daily drive from home to your regular office or shop. That is commuting, even if you take calls on the way. Log it as Commuting so the miles are accounted for.

Other driving

Account for the rest of the miles: commuting, personal errands, medical appointments, charity work, and other drivers. Totals are fine here. Anything you don't explain stays unaccounted; it is never assumed to be business or personal.

Car costs (optional)

If you have your actual car costs for the year, enter them so your tax pro can compare the standard mileage rate with actual costs. Enter the full-year amounts; the calculator applies your business-use percentage. Parking and tolls for business trips go on each trip, since they count under either method.
