Fix-it check

Rules that make it hold up

  • The child does real, age-appropriate work for the business, and you keep timesheets and job descriptions.
  • Pay is reasonable for the work, what you'd pay someone else for the same job.
  • Pay goes to the child's own account, from the business account that's running the payroll.
  • Run it as payroll with a W-4 and a W-2, even when Social Security and Medicare don't apply. File the quarterly and annual returns.
  • The exemption depends on who the employer is. A sole proprietorship, a single-member LLC that isn't taxed as a corporation, or a partnership owned only by the child's parents qualifies. A corporation (S or C), any other partnership, or an estate doesn't.
  • Up to the standard deduction ($16,100 in 2026), a child's wages carry no federal income tax. The child can also put earned income, up to $7,500, into a Roth IRA.
