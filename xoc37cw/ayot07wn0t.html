Ways to structure your business and assets

There is no one right answer. The best setup depends on your goals, your state, how much risk your business carries, and your family plans. These are the options we walk through together.

Example structures

Two common setups, drawn simply. Your own structure is designed around your goals during your structuring session.

Good to know

Keep it separate

Personal spending on a business card, or business bills paid from your personal account, weakens the protection your LLC gives you. If it happens, track it on your personally paid expenses sheet so we can record it properly.

Is an S corporation right for me?

Sometimes, once profit is steady. It adds payroll and more paperwork, so the savings have to be worth the work. We run your real numbers in the S Corp Pay Planner before recommending it.

Revocable or irrevocable trust?

A revocable trust can be changed any time and keeps things out of probate court, but it does not shield assets from lawsuits. An irrevocable trust gives stronger protection, but you give up control. Your attorney drafts the trust. We coordinate the tax side.

Rentals usually go in an LLC

Rental properties are usually held in LLCs rather than an S corporation. That keeps more flexibility for depreciation and for passing the property to your heirs. Before you retitle a property, check with your lender and your insurance company.

One structure does not fit forever

As your income, family, or properties change, your structure should too. We review it with you at least once a year as part of your planning.

Save every official letter

Your IRS EIN letter, state approval, and any notices are the records banks, lenders, and the IRS ask for. Upload them to your secure portal so they are never lost.

Stay in good standing every year

Most states ask LLCs to file a yearly report, and many charge a yearly fee or franchise tax to keep the business active. Miss it, and the state can add penalties or even close the business on paper.
