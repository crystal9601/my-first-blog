Sales tax and use tax in plain English

Sales tax

Tax you charge your customer when you sell something taxable. You hold it for the state and pay it with your return. Whether a sale is taxable depends on what you sold, where the sale took place, and any exemption that applies.

Example: a customer buys a $40 costume, you charge tax on top, and that tax goes to the state.

Use tax

Tax you owe yourself when you buy something taxable for your business and the seller did not charge sales tax. It is the same tax, paid by you instead of collected by the seller.

Example: an online supplier ships you cleaning supplies with no tax on the receipt. You may need to report use tax on them.

Nexus

A word for "enough connection to a state that you may have to register and collect its sales tax on taxable sales." A store, employees or stored inventory there can count. So can selling enough to customers there, even from out of state.

Example: a Charlotte shop that ships to many Georgia customers may owe Georgia sales tax.

        Buying to resell

        1. You buy the product to resell.
        2. You give your vendor a resale certificate (a form that says you will resell it).
        3. The vendor does not charge you sales tax on that purchase.
        4. You sell the product to your customer.
        5. You charge sales tax when required.
        6. You report and pay that tax to your state.

        Buying to use in your business

        1. You buy something you use yourself, like supplies used during a service.
        2. The vendor should charge sales tax if the item is taxable.
        3. If they did not, use tax may be owed on it.
        4. The receipt shows which one happened.

        STEP 4Do you sell into other states?Example figures

        This step still has example figures. They are not your information.

        You generally collect sales tax on taxable sales in the state where your business is located. You may also need to register in another state if you have a location, employees or stored inventory there, or if your sales to customers there pass that state's limit.

        Add each state where you have customers, with your sales to that state for the current or past year. Most limits are $100,000, but some are higher and some also count the number of sales. States differ on which sales count (for example, sales through marketplaces like Amazon or Etsy, or sales that are not taxable) and on the time period they measure. This check is a first look, not a final answer on whether you must register.

        STEP 5Your sales tax filings

        Once you are registered, the state expects a return for every period it assigns you, even a month with no sales. The state decides how often you file, so check your registration letter or state account if you are not sure.

        We do not show due dates here, because they depend on the filing schedule your state assigned you. We will confirm yours.

        STEP 6Get receipts into QuickBooks

        Your bookkeeping tells us what was recorded. The receipt or invoice shows what actually happened. Please keep both in QuickBooks Online, where they connect to the transaction. You do not need to email receipts to us.

        How to do this on your phone or computer

        On your phone

        1. Open the QuickBooks app and sign in.
        2. Find the receipt capture option (the camera or Receipts area).
        3. Take a clear picture of the whole receipt.
        4. Check that it matched to the right bank or card charge.

        On your computer

        1. Sign in to QuickBooks Online and go to the Receipts area.
        2. Upload or drag in PDF receipts and invoices.
        3. Find your receipt forwarding email address there and save it as a contact.
        4. In your email, set a forwarding rule for vendors who bill you every month.

        Menu names in QuickBooks change from time to time. If you cannot find these, respond to our email and we will walk you through it.
